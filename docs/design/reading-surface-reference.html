Phase 02 · Logo Direction

A Mark for Steadfield

Logo Direction Agent · 14 June 2026 · v1

A mark that looks like it has always existed. Not a brand announcing itself, but a name that has been earning its place for decades.

Opening

What the foundation asks for

Steadfield needs a mark that reads as established, not introduced. The work is not to invent an identity. It is to give shape to one the brand has already earned.

The foundation gives a clear instruction: field guide, not deck. The logo carries information quietly. It does not perform.

Reading the foundation

Three things stand out

First, the Sage archetype asks for authority that is given, not claimed. Second, the paradox, ancient patience and modern tools, is genuinely productive tension, not a copywriting flourish.

Third, the Visual DNA is already decisive: field green as anchor, parchment as carrier, ochre as the single warm signal, soil ink for type. The type note, a serif display with quiet humanist body, points toward a wordmark that earns its weight through proportion, not decoration.

Briefing your designer

How to hand this off

Hand the designer all three directions as distinct briefs, not as a ranked list. Ask for two to three rough sketches per direction, at wordmark size and at favicon size, in one color only first. Reserve color application for a second round.

Refuse sketches that introduce new typefaces outside the serif display and humanist body pairing already established. Test each one with a single question: does it look like it belongs in a well-set field guide, or does it look like it launched last quarter. Only the former passes.

The work

Three directions

01The Field Edition

Takes the Sage archetype literally. The wordmark is set in a confident serif display, unhurried in its spacing, the kind of letterform you find on a botanical press plate or a land survey. It reads like a named thing, a place or a publication, not a product. The ochre accent appears in a single deliberate moment, a ruled line or a period, as punctuation rather than decoration.

Qualities

  • Tight-tracked serif wordmark, generous leading if stacked
  • Single ochre rule or stop mark as the only accent
  • Parchment ground implied by the weight of the letters, not filled
  • No symbol, the name carries everything
  • Ink quality in the strokes, slight optical variation, not digital-smooth
02Measured Growth

Takes the archetype laterally, away from the editorial and into the agricultural. The mark borrows the visual language of a field cross-section or a growth index, a simple geometric form that reads as accumulation over time, rings or intervals, drawn in soil ink on parchment. The wordmark sits below it in a quieter weight, secondary to the symbol for once. This is the direction that resolves toward the Creator, building something visible.

Qualities

  • Abstract symbol suggesting strata, rings, or measured intervals
  • Symbol in soil ink, mark in field green
  • Wordmark set small and lowercase beneath, humanist serif
  • Generous negative space inside and around the symbol
  • Scales to a single-color stamp without losing legibility
03Patient Tension

Lives in the paradox directly: ancient patience, modern tools. The wordmark is the same confident serif, but the construction is interrupted, one letter optically corrected or one stroke drawn with the precision of a technical instrument rather than a calligrapher's hand. It reads as old material handled by a careful modern mind. The tension lives in the letterform itself, not in color or ornament.

Qualities

  • Primarily serif wordmark with one deliberate geometric intervention
  • Intervention in ochre, everything else in soil ink
  • No symbol, the wordmark is the whole argument
  • Stroke contrast that references both classical type and drafting tools
  • Works at small scale without the intervention becoming noise
The guardrails

Always and never

Always

  • Use the established palette: field green, parchment, ochre, soil ink
  • Give the wordmark room to breathe
  • Favor weight and proportion over ornament
  • Let the serif carry authority without help from effects
  • Test every sketch against the phrase: does it look unhurried

Never

  • Use neon purple in any form, including adjacent violet
  • Add gradients, shadows, or dimensional effects
  • Set the wordmark in a condensed display-only face that reads aggressive
  • Crowd the mark with a tagline at lockup stage
  • Let any direction read as a tech product or productivity tool
