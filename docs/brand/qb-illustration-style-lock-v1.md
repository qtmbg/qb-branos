# QB Illustration Style Lock
## The canonical specification for every illustration in the Quantum Branding universe.
### v1.0 · April 2026

This document defines the visual rules for every character, scene, and object inside QB BrandOS. It is the authority that any human illustrator, generative model, or production team answers to when adding to the QB illustration library.

It contains no recommendations. It states what is. Any deviation is an error.

---

## 01 · Aesthetic

Modern editorial vector illustration. Calm, playful, intelligent, slightly whimsical. Scandinavian and contemporary design sensibility. The work feels print-ready and intentional, never improvised.

No realism. No painterly textures. No sketchy hand-drawn wobble. No 3D effects. No photorealism. No hyper-detail. No noise. No grain.

Everything in the QB illustration universe looks like it was drawn by the same designer, on the same day, by the same hand, with zero stylistic deviation.

---

## 02 · Line and shape language

Uniform, clean, slightly rounded vector outlines. Stroke weight is consistent across the entire illustration. Lines never thicken or thin within a single drawing.

Shapes are simple, bold, and softly geometric. Curves are preferred over sharp angles. Symbolic over detailed. Suggestive over explicit.

Outlines are always drawn in QB ink (#2D1521), the same deep aubergine that carries body text and 2px borders across the QB visual system. The illustration outlines and the system borders are the same colour. This is the visual handshake that ties the illustrations to the cream-and-ink chassis.

---

## 03 · Colour system

The illustration palette is locked. Seven colours. Every QB illustration draws from this set and no other.

Forest #5B7E6A. Peach #E89380. Coral #DC6B52. Mustard #D4B85A. Rust #B8704D. Lavender #B8A0C7. Pink-soft #F4C4D0.

Colours are flat fills only. No shading. No highlights. No gradients. No textures. Skin tones are stylized and uniform, not realistic. Black appears sparingly: outlines, small accents, occasional clothing details.

The illustration palette pairs with the QB system surfaces. Cream backgrounds (#FBF5E6) hold the figures. Cream-card frames (#F2EBD3) hold the illustrations inside the system's cards. Aubergine ink (#2D1521) carries the linework.

The seven illustration colours never replace the system roles. Gold (#E0B069) stays for primary CTAs. Rose (#CA6180) stays for the brand-mark motif. Teal (#9ED3DC) stays for focus and selection. Phase colours stay for tags and dots. The illustration palette is for paint inside the drawings, not for system roles outside them.

Saturated neon, fluorescent tones, and high-key colour are absent. The QB universe is muted, dusty, calm.

---

## 04 · Character design

Characters have simplified anatomy. Limbs are slightly elongated and soft. Hands and feet are minimal and stylized.

Faces are symbolic. Small noses. Minimal eyes drawn as dots, lines, or simple shapes. Mouths are simple curves. Expressions are subtle, friendly, never theatrical.

Hair is drawn as solid shape, not as strands. Clothing is graphic and blocky with simple folds. Accessories (hats, glasses, bags, instruments) are stylized and intentional.

Characters never carry hyper-detailed elements. No fingernails, no eyelashes, no individual teeth, no jewellery beads, no logo prints on garments. The QB universe lives at one level of abstraction throughout.

Five named characters anchor the universe.

The Blank Slate. A solo skater with coffee, drink in hand. The figure of fresh starts. Door 01 of the Four Doors.
The Doubter. A seated figure at a café table, hand on chin, contemplative. The figure of the founder questioning what is built. Door 02.
The Player. A runner with a dog on leash, forward motion. The figure of momentum. Door 03.
The Multi-Brand. A figure holding an oversized framed portrait. The figure of the agency operator carrying multiple brands. Door 04.
The Guide. A figure on a tandem bicycle, two riders. The figure of partnership and journey.

These five are the canonical characters. Additions to the library follow the same anatomy, line, and colour rules.

---

## 05 · Perspective and composition

Perspective is mostly flat or lightly isometric. No deep perspective. No realistic depth.

Objects feel placed, not rendered. Scenes feel balanced and airy. Negative space is intentional and clean. The composition reads at a glance and rewards a second look.

Characters and objects sit at human scale unless the illustration is intentionally surreal (oversized framed portrait, oversized hands, oversized props for editorial effect). When scale is exaggerated, it is exaggerated boldly, not subtly.

---

## 06 · Environment and objects

Buildings, furniture, plants, and props follow the same rules as characters. Stylized. Geometric. Slightly exaggerated.

Repetition of shapes creates rhythm. A row of windows. A stack of books. A grid of plants. The eye finds pattern in the composition.

Details are symbolic, not literal. A coffee cup is a curve and a handle. A laptop is a rectangle and a screen rectangle. A bicycle is two circles and a frame.

---

## 07 · Lighting and texture

No realistic lighting. No directional shadows. No ambient occlusion. No rim light. No bloom.

Shadows appear only when graphic and intentional, used as a flat shape that adds compositional weight, never as physical light simulation.

No textures. No noise. No grain. No paper texture overlays. The work reads as clean vector, print-ready.

---

## 08 · Mood and narrative

The QB universe is lively but calm. Collaborative. Human-centred. Playful, never chaotic.

Characters are doing things: working, walking, running, thinking, gathering, building. The illustrations tell stories without resorting to realism. The viewer fills in the rest.

The mood matches the QB voice. Direct. Calm. Slightly contrarian. Aware of weight. Trustful of the reader. The illustrations and the words live in the same emotional register.

---

## 09 · Strict exclusions

The following are absent from QB illustrations. Their presence is an error.

Photorealism.
Gradients of any kind.
3D rendering or perspective tricks.
Hyper-detail (fingernails, eyelashes, individual teeth, watch faces, screen text, brand logos on objects).
Painterly or sketch styles.
Harsh shadows or directional lighting.
Neon, fluorescent, or oversaturated colour.
Stock-illustration tropes (giant smartphones with text bubbles flying out, crowds of identical figures pointing at charts, lightbulb moments above heads).
AI-generated tells (random tabletop objects, garbled text on signs, mismatched eye sizes, six-fingered hands, "Hola Hallo Salut" speech-bubble pile-ups).
Watermarks, signatures, or visible artist marks.

## 11 · Production rules

New illustrations follow these production rules.

Every new illustration is rendered against the master prompt above. The scene description is added at the end. The style block is not edited.

Every new illustration is reviewed against the five named canonical characters before approval. If the new figure looks like it could stand next to The Blank Slate, The Doubter, The Player, The Multi-Brand, and The Guide without visible style breaks, it ships. If it looks foreign, it does not.

Every new illustration uses the locked seven-colour palette. Adding a new colour to the universe requires updating this document first. Otherwise, the colour is an error.

Every new illustration is delivered as inline SVG with `currentColor` on outlines where possible, or as PNG with transparent background at minimum 1200px on the longest side.

Every new illustration is named consistently: kebab-case, descriptive of the character or scene, with no version numbers in the filename. Versions live in the repo, not the asset name.

## 12 · The canonical generator

The QB Character Machine is the operational implementation of this Style Lock. It is the canonical method for adding new illustrations to the QB universe.

The machine accepts a character description, a scene context, and an optional reference image. It returns an illustration rendered against the locked QB style.

Text-to-image generations route through Imagen. Image-to-image transformations route through Gemini Flash Image. Both pipelines are constrained by the master prompt in Section 10. The style block is not editable at runtime. The scene description is the only variable.

The machine lives inside QB BrandOS Phase 02 Brand Creation. Its output is delivered inside the qb-illus-card frame, the same cream-card chassis that holds every other QB illustration. The handshake with the QB visual system is automatic.

Illustrations not produced by the Character Machine are still acceptable. They follow the same Style Lock. The machine is the canonical path. It is not the only path.

## 13 · How illustrations sit in the QB system

Illustrations sit inside cream-card frames with the QB hard offset shadow. They never appear as bare floating images in the layout.

The cream-card frame uses cream-card surface (#F2EBD3), 2px ink border, and the system's hard offset shadow (0 9px var(--ink) on mobile, 0 16px var(--ink) on desktop and up). The illustration sits inside that frame.

The frame is documented in the QB Design System under the qb-illus-card pattern.

Illustrations support both static and motion variants. Static is PNG or inline SVG. Motion is short MOV or MP4 video, hover-played, looping silently. Motion variants always carry a static fallback.

The illustration system and the brand mark are two distinct visual systems. They coexist. They never replace each other.

The brand mark is the primary identifier of Quantum Branding. Geometric. Hand-drawn editorial character. Three strokes. Four colourways. The mark and wordmark lock together in the navigation, the footer, the favicon, and on every official surface where the brand asserts ownership. The mark identifies.

The illustration system is the editorial visual language of QB BrandOS. Hand-drawn character. Seven flat fills. Character-forward composition. Illustrations appear in cards, on pages where the brand explains what it does, in tools where the system speaks to the user. The illustrations narrate.

A single page can carry the brand mark and an illustration on the same view. They sit in different roles. The mark is identity. The illustration is story. Both speak the same visual language: ink #2D1521 outlines, hand-drawn character, deliberate restraint.

The full brand mark specification lives in the QB Design System.
