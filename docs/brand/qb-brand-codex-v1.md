## 07 · The visual language

The visual identity is built on six signatures that hold the system together.

Cream and ink. Cream surfaces (#FBF5E6). Deep aubergine ink (#2D1521) for type, borders, and the brand mark. No grey middle ground. No dark mode default.

The two-layer 3D pill button. A solid ink slab behind. A coloured content layer in front. The two layers move at different rates on hover and press. The motion is slow, confident, deliberate.

The hard offset shadow. Cards, illustrations, and product mockups carry a solid offset shadow with no blur and full opacity. The visual signature is sticker-stamped tactile, not soft drop-shadow modern.

The eyebrow tag plus serif headline rhythm. Every section opens with a small monospaced uppercase tag, followed by a Fraunces headline with an italic gold spotlight phrase.

Fluid clamp type and space. Nothing is a fixed pixel. The system breathes between viewport widths.

Editorial illustrations inside cream-card frames. Bold ink outlines. Flat colour fills. Character-forward composition. The illustrations carry their own palette and sit inside the cream system like prints inside a magazine.

The full visual specification lives in the QB Design System.

## 08 · The brand mark

The QB mark reads as infinity at a glance. As Q plus B on closer inspection.

Two lobes meeting at a central crossing. The left lobe is round, an enclosed Q loop. The right lobe is articulated with a central pinch, a B figure on its side. A short descending tail clarifies the Q. Without the tail, the mark is generic infinity. The tail is the design.

Three strokes. One continuous reading. Hand-drawn editorial character.

The mark exists in four colourways. Ink default. Gold for premium and editorial moments. Rose for emotive contexts. Reverse cream-on-ink for dark backgrounds only. Nothing else.

The wordmark is "quantum branding" set in Fraunces italic, weight 600, SOFT axis 60, lowercase. Always lowercase. Even at the start of a sentence. Even in headlines.

The mark and the wordmark together form the primary identifier. They lock in two configurations. Horizontal lockup: mark left, wordmark right, baseline-aligned. Vertical lockup: mark above, wordmark below, centred.

Clear space around the mark equals the wordmark x-height. Nothing crosses that boundary. No badges stacked into it. No taglines bolted underneath. No alternate marks exist.

The mark is locked. The wordmark is locked. The lockups are locked.

## 10 · The illustrations

Quantum Branding's illustration language is editorial vector. Flat, calm, intelligent, slightly whimsical. Scandinavian and contemporary design sensibility. No realism. No painterly textures. No 3D effects.

Characters have simplified anatomy. Slightly elongated soft limbs. Minimal hands and feet. Faces are symbolic: small noses, minimal eyes, simple curve mouths. Expressions are subtle and friendly. Hair is solid shape, not strands.

Lines are uniform, clean, slightly rounded. Stroke weight is consistent. No sketchy wobble. Shapes are simple, bold, softly geometric. Curves win over sharp angles.

Colours are flat fills from the locked illustration palette. No shading. No highlights. No gradients. Skin tones are stylized and uniform, not realistic. Black is used sparingly: outlines, accents, occasional clothing.

Composition is flat or lightly isometric. No deep perspective. Objects feel placed, not rendered. Negative space is intentional. Scenes feel balanced and airy.

Buildings, furniture, and objects follow the same rules as characters. Stylized. Geometric. Slightly exaggerated. Repetition of shapes creates rhythm. Details are symbolic, not realistic.

The mood is lively but calm. Collaborative. Human-centred. Playful, never chaotic. The illustration tells a story without realism.

The full illustration style lock, with the master prompt for generating new characters in this universe, lives in the QB Illustration Style Lock document.

The canonical method for generating new QB illustrations is the QB Character Machine. It is the operational implementation of the Style Lock. Every output passes through the same locked palette, the same line behaviour, and the same compositional rules.
